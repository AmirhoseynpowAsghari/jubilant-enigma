# HHM self-consistent grid results

Generated by `../run_hhm_grid.py` with `U=90t`, `Delta_t=4.5t`, `n=1.875`,
`V_SO/t = 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8`, `Nk = 80, 160, 320`, and requested
`T/Tc = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 0.99` (90 finite-T rows; 15 parameter/grid
summaries). The legacy gap-fit chemical potential is evaluated at the same absolute `T` as
the HHM state, using the legacy proxy's own `Tc`, rather than at the same normalized
fraction.

## Verified numerical checks

- All 15 determinant roots are invariant across `Nk=80,160,320` to the displayed numerical
  precision (maximum spread `1.1e-15` in the exported Tc values).
- The largest coupled-equation residual infinity norm among the 90 requested states is
  `1.43e-14`. Each T=0 point's chosen nontrivial root has residual at most `2.22e-16`.
- At fixed SOC and temperature fraction, `D0`, `DS`, and `mu_SC` vary by less than about
  `1e-12` between `Nk=80` and `Nk=320` in these runs. `Deff` differs by up to `4.8e-6`;
  the finite-grid T=0 normal chemical-potential *seed* can differ by about `2.0e-3`, because
  the T=0 normal occupation is a discrete step and `find_mu_normal` intentionally uses a
  quantile rather than treating that seed as a unique root.
- Across this grid, the legacy 1%-effective-gap proxy lies below the determinant definition
  by `3.77e-5` to `4.02e-4` in absolute `t` units (relative offsets from the determinant
  `Tc` range from `-0.0941%` to `-0.00520%` on this grid). This is a comparison of
  definitions, not evidence either definition is more stable.
- The largest absolute difference between fitted-gap `mu` and fully self-consistent `mu_SC`
  among these matched-absolute-temperature rows is `6.58e-3 t` (at the high-SOC end).
  The CSV separately records `mu_SC`, normal `mu_N`, `mu_fitted_gap`, and the fitted-gap
  evaluation fraction so zero-temperature chemical-potential offsets are not conflated with
  the additional fitted-gap deviation.
- The multistart T=0 search returned one nontrivial residual-verified candidate for each of
  these 15 points. Finite-T checkpoint diagnostics record the selected superconducting root
  and any separately found trivial/near-normal root. This is evidence for the tested seed
  sets, not a proof of global uniqueness. Rerun with `--initial-root-index` when multiple
  accepted T=0 roots are present; an out-of-range index is rejected explicitly.

## Files and limits

- `hhm_self_consistent_grid.csv`: requested observables and fitted-gap comparison.
- `hhm_self_consistent_summary.json`: determinant roots, candidate roots, residuals, and
  finite-T alternative-root diagnostics.
- `hhm_gap_shape_grid_summary.csv`: complete-grid, per-helicity min/max/mean/RMS and sign
  fraction of `bar-Delta_{k,s}(T)` for every requested state; this is a compact distribution
  summary of the calculated k-dependent gap.
- `hhm_gap_shape_high_symmetry_samples.csv`: `bar-Delta_{k,s}` at six explicitly labeled
  high-symmetry/intermediate momenta, for both helicities and every state.
- `tc_grid_convergence.png`, `finite_t_observables.png`, and `hhm_gap_shape_statistics.png`:
  calculations only; the finite-T figure displays `D0`, `DS`, and `mu_SC-mu_N`, while the
  gap-shape plot shows the positive-helicity RMS with grid min/max shading.

**Published-reference limitation:** the cited paper's complete temperature-dependent
`D0(T)`, `DS(T)`, `mu(T)`, and `bar-Delta_{k,s}(T)` curves have not been digitized into
numerical reference data here. These outputs therefore do not demonstrate quantitative
reproduction of Fig. 8's full curves. The plotted curves are not overlaid on invented paper
data; only the existing independent determinant helper provides a direct numerical
cross-check at the tested point in the automated test.

No free-energy functional or fixed-filling free-energy comparison is implemented. Root
residuals, convergence, and effective-gap size establish neither uniqueness nor
thermodynamic stability.
